# User research

Help plan ethical, focused research that answers a decision-relevant question. Distinguish planning recommendations from research actually conducted. Do not recruit participants, contact users, access customer records, or collect data unless the caller explicitly requests it and an authorized capability is actually available.

## Method selection

Sample ranges and timelines below are rough planning heuristics, not guarantees, statistical requirements, or promises of representativeness. Adapt them to the question, population, accessibility needs, recruitment feasibility, and analysis method.

| Method | Useful for | Typical planning scale (not a rule) | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| User interviews | Needs, context, motivations, mental models | Often 5–8 exploratory sessions | Not population prevalence |
| Usability testing | Finding task-flow friction in a specific design | Often 5–8 iterative sessions | Small samples do not establish population rates |
| Surveys | Measuring stated attitudes/behaviors across a defined sample | Sample size depends on population, sampling, precision, and response rate; do not default to a fixed threshold | Nonresponse, wording, and sampling bias |
| Card sorting | Exploring information architecture groupings | Often 15–30 exploratory participants | Results depend on task and participant mix |
| Diary studies | Behavior and context over time | Often 10–15 participants over weeks | Burden, attrition, and self-report bias |
| A/B experiments | Comparing defined variants on a measurable outcome | Power and duration require baseline, effect size, variance, and design assumptions | Confounding, novelty, and ethics; do not infer causal impact without valid design |

## Plan structure

1. **Decision and objectives**: What decision will findings inform? What is explicitly out of scope?
2. **Research questions**: Neutral, answerable questions; avoid leading wording.
3. **Method rationale**: Why the method matches the question; what it cannot answer.
4. **Participants**: Relevant inclusion/exclusion criteria and recruitment channel, subject to consent, privacy, and authorization. Never invent a user population or promise access.
5. **Protocol**: Tasks/questions, moderator prompts, timing, recording/consent plan, accessibility accommodations, and safety boundaries.
6. **Analysis plan**: Observation capture, coding/affinity approach, quantitative denominator/statistical plan when applicable, and how counterexamples will be handled.
7. **Risks and logistics**: Privacy/data minimization, incentives if supplied, dependencies, schedule ranges, and approvals needed.

## Interview guide structure

A flexible guide may include: welcome/consent and purpose; context and current workflow; non-leading deep-dive questions; concept/prototype reaction (clearly label hypothetical material); wrap-up and participant questions. Time boxes are planning estimates. Use neutral probes such as “What happened next?” rather than suggesting an answer.

## Analysis frameworks

- **Affinity mapping**: Group traceable observations, retain source references and divergent cases.
- **Impact/effort matrix**: Rank opportunities with explicit, provisional assumptions rather than false precision.
- **Journey mapping**: Show stages, actions, context, needs, and evidence; label gaps and hypotheses.
- **Jobs to Be Done**: Describe the situation, motivation, desired progress, and constraints only as supported by participant evidence.

## Deliverables

- Research plan: decision, objectives, questions, method, participants, logistics, risks, timeline assumptions.
- Interview/usability guide: consent, tasks/questions, neutral probes, and completion criteria.
- Survey: construct, neutral items, response options, order, accessibility, and analysis plan.
- Synthesis plan/report: source traceability, themes, insights, limitations, and next questions.
- Highlight reel outline: selected exact quotes/observations with permission and source identifiers.
